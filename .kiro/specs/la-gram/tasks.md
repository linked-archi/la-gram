### Task 22: Large models — performance (recorded 2026-10-24) — done (2026-10-25)
Measured in Chrome on the built app with three models — 34 000 triples (Archisurance: 360 elements, 600 relationships, 138 views), 65 000 (3 300 elements, one view of 9 277 shapes laid out on open) and 347 000 (3 600 elements, 6 700 relationships, 1 126 views): opening took 18 s, 23 s and 106 s, the UI frozen for up to 11 s, 18 s and 66 s at a time; one rename froze it for 9.8 s, 3.9 s and 61 s; the heap reached 1.2 GB. Parsing is not the cost (0.3 s for 27 MB). The causes, measured:
1. *Validation after every change*, of the whole model, on the main thread — 9 of the 9.8 s and 54 of the 61 s; of it, 86 % the generated relationship shapes, whose `sh:or` of permitted pairs makes rdf-validate-shacl build and discard a result per failing pair (14.1 s with them, 1.9 s without; 0.6.5 no faster)
2. *The store read whole and often*: each query crosses into Oxigraph's WebAssembly and converts every result; `modelQuads()` copies the model for validation, the tree, opening and the checkpoint comparison; a view's nodes are read with ~8 queries each — in Node, opening the largest model spent 378 of 420 s collecting garbage
3. *Whole panels rebuilt on every change*, synchronously: the model tree from a full copy of the model (5.4 s on opening the 65 000-triple model), the diagram re-synced, the property panel reloaded
4. *A bug*: a route whose `rdf:List` cells are blank nodes (the converters write them) failed to read — the cell was re-made as a named node, which Oxigraph refuses — so such a model stopped opening half-way in the browser
